A Food Culture That Refuses to Modernize (Thank God)

Built in the 17th century by Shah Jahan's daughter Jahanara Begum, Chandni Chowk was designed as a moonlit square. Today, the canal is gone, but the moonlight magic lives on in trendy, popular street foods that have been made the exact same way for hundreds of years.

What makes the food culture here so addictive isn't just the taste — it's the ritual. Once established recipes are family secrets passed down like heirlooms.

Century-old jalebis sizzling in pure desi ghee at Dariba Kalan corner.

The Legends: Old Shops That Built Delhi's Appetite

You can't talk about Chandni Chowk without naming the popular icons that have fed generations:

  • Paranthe Wali Gali – An entire lane dedicated to trendy stuffed paranthas since the 1870s.
  • Karim's – Near Jama Masjid, serving popular Mughlai kebabs and curries since 1913[cite: 6].
  • Old Famous Jalebi Wala – A century-old jalebi institution at Dariba Kalan corner[cite: 6].
  • Natraj Dahi Bhalle – Serving popular dahi bhalle since 1940[cite: 6].

The Menu: Every Speciality You Need to Hunt Down

Chandni Chowk offers trendy and popular bites that define capital gastronomy:
