HERITAGE SPOTS

Sunsets at Mughal Gardens & Hidden Tombs

There's a version of Delhi that only shows up after 5 pm — when the harsh afternoon sun softens into gold, the crowds thin out, and centuries-old sandstone starts glowing like it's lit from within.

This is the Delhi locals chase on weekends: a city where every monument doubles as the perfect sunset backdrop, and every "just a quick visit" turns into an hour of quietly staring at history. Grab your walking shoes and your metro card — we're doing a full golden-hour heritage crawl through Delhi's most iconic (and a few criminally underrated) spots.

1.

Mughal Gardens: The Sunset Star of the List

Tucked inside the President's Estate, the Mughal Gardens (now renamed Amrit Udyan) are Delhi's best-kept secret for a reason — they're only open to the public for a limited season each year, usually between February and March. When you do catch them, it's magic: manicured lawns, canals inspired by Mughal design, and flowerbeds so vivid they look painted. Come at sunset, and the entire garden turns into a soft golden canvas.

🚇 Nearest Metro: Central Secretariat (Yellow & Violet Line)
🚶 How to reach: From Central Secretariat station, it's roughly a 15-20 minute walk or a quick auto ride to the Rashtrapati Bhavan gate. Entry is via online booking during the open season, so plan ahead.
2.

Red Fort: Where Sunset Meets Empire

No Delhi heritage list is complete without the massive red sandstone walls of Red Fort (Lal Qila), Shah Jahan's 17th-century seat of power. Watching the sun dip behind its ramparts while Chandni Chowk buzzes just outside is a full sensory experience — history in front of you, street food aroma behind you.

🚇 Nearest Metro: Lal Qila (Violet Line)
🚶 How to reach: The station is a two-minute walk from the fort's entrance — about as convenient as heritage travel gets in Delhi.
3.

Jama Masjid: Sunset Prayers and Skyline Views

Right around the corner from Red Fort stands Jama Masjid, one of India's largest mosques, built by the same Mughal emperor. Climb the southern minaret (small fee applies) right before sunset, and you get a sweeping view of Old Delhi's rooftops turning amber, with the call to prayer echoing below.

🚇 Nearest Metro: Jama Masjid (Violet Line)
🚶 How to reach: A short 5-minute walk from the metro station gets you right to the mosque's steps.
4.

Purana Qila: The Underrated Sunset Gem

While everyone rushes to Red Fort, Purana Qila — believed to stand on the site of the legendary city of Indraprastha — stays peacefully uncrowded. Its massive stone walls and the boat club by the moat make it one of the most romantic, least-hyped sunset spots in the city.

🚇 Nearest Metro: Pragati Maidan (Blue Line)
🚶 How to reach: About a 10-minute walk from Pragati Maidan station takes you straight to its gates.
5.

Humayun's Tomb: The Blueprint for the Taj Mahal

Often called the precursor to the Taj Mahal, Humayun's Tomb is a UNESCO World Heritage Site with a symmetrical charbagh garden that looks stunning any time of day — but at sunset, the reddish sandstone practically glows. It's peaceful, spacious, and criminally underrated compared to its more famous cousin in Agra.

🚇 Nearest Metro: JLN Stadium (Violet Line) or Jangpura (Pink Line)
🚶 How to reach: Roughly a 15-20 minute walk or a short auto ride from either station.
6.

Safdarjung's Tomb: Delhi's "Hidden" Tomb

If Humayun's Tomb is the famous sibling, Safdarjung's Tomb is the quiet, underrated one — and honestly, that's what makes it special. Built in the mid-1700s, this marble-and-sandstone mausoleum sits in its own peaceful garden, with far fewer crowds and just as much charm. Sunset here feels almost private.

🚇 Nearest Metro: Jor Bagh (Yellow Line)
🚶 How to reach: About a 10-minute walk from Jor Bagh station.
7.

Qutub Minar: The Tallest Sunset View in Delhi

Standing at 73 metres, Qutub Minar is the tallest brick minaret in the world, surrounded by ruins, arches, and the mysterious Iron Pillar that hasn't rusted in over 1,600 years. As the sun sets behind the tower, the entire complex takes on a dramatic, almost cinematic look.

🚇 Nearest Metro: Qutub Minar (Yellow Line)
🚶 How to reach: A quick 10-minute walk or auto ride from the station.
8.

Lodhi Gardens: Sunset, Joggers, and Ancient Tombs Together

Lodhi Gardens is where Delhi comes to unwind — joggers, picnickers, couples, and 15th-century Lodhi and Sayyid dynasty tombs all sharing the same green space. Watching the sun set behind the Bara Gumbad or Sheesh Gumbad, framed by manicured lawns, is one of the most peaceful experiences in the city.

🚇 Nearest Metro: Jor Bagh (Yellow Line) or Khan Market (Violet Line)
🚶 How to reach: Around a 10-15 minute walk from either station.
9.

India Gate: The Classic Evening Hangout

You can't talk about Delhi sunsets without India Gate. This 42-metre war memorial, lit up against a fading orange sky, is where the entire city seems to gather for evening walks, ice cream, and boat rides on the adjoining lawns. It's touristy, sure — but there's a reason it never gets old.

🚇 Nearest Metro: Central Secretariat (Yellow & Violet Line)
🚶 How to reach: About a 15-minute walk, or hop in an auto for a quicker ride straight to the lawns.
10.

Jantar Mantar: Sunset Over Ancient Science

An 18th-century astronomical observatory built by Maharaja Jai Singh II, Jantar Mantar is full of quirky, oversized stone instruments once used to track the stars and time. At sunset, the geometric structures cast long, dramatic shadows — an underrated photo-op hiding in plain sight near Connaught Place.

🚇 Nearest Metro: Patel Chowk (Yellow Line)
🚶 How to reach: A short 5-10 minute walk from the station.
11.

Lotus Temple: Sunset Petals in Full Glow

Shaped like a blooming lotus flower, the Bahá'í Lotus Temple looks striking at any hour, but sunset adds a warm glow to its white marble petals that makes it look almost otherworldly. It's a place of quiet reflection, open to all faiths, so keep your visit calm and peaceful.

🚇 Nearest Metro: Kalkaji Mandir (Magenta & Violet Line)
🚶 How to reach: About a 10-minute walk from the station.
12.

Akshardham Temple: The Grand Finale

Save this one for a slightly later evening — Akshardham Temple hosts a spectacular musical fountain show after dark, alongside intricately carved sandstone and marble architecture that's illuminated beautifully once the sun goes down. It's less "ancient ruins" and more "modern architectural marvel," but it earns its spot on any Delhi heritage trail.

🚇 Nearest Metro: Akshardham (Blue Line)
🚶 How to reach: The station is directly connected to the temple complex via a short walkway.

Your Golden Hour Game Plan

Here's the fun part — Delhi's metro network connects almost every single one of these spots, so you can genuinely string together a heritage-hopping evening without touching a car. Pick two or three that are metro-close to each other (Humayun's Tomb + Safdarjung's Tomb + Lodhi Gardens is a solid golden-hour trio), time your last stop right before sunset, and just let the city's history glow around you.

"Delhi doesn't just have monuments — it has moods, and sunset is easily its best one. So next weekend, skip the mall, grab a friend, and let the city's tombs, gardens, and forts remind you why golden hour was invented right here."
