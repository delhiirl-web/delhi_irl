Lodhi Art District: Delhi's Open-Air Gallery You Can Walk Through

By Delhi IRL • 4 Min Read • Updated August 2026

Forget everything you know about visiting an art gallery — the hushed silence, the "do not touch" signs, the entry tickets. Now imagine strolling down an ordinary Delhi residential street and suddenly finding a five-storey building wearing a giant mural of a woman lost in thought.

That's Lodhi Colony for you — except everyone calls it the Lodhi Art District, India's first open-air public art district, and it's basically a treasure hunt disguised as a walk.

How a Government Colony Became an Art Capital

Here's the twist nobody expects: Lodhi Art District isn't in some trendy, curated art hub — it's a government housing colony built in the 1940s, full of quiet lanes and boxy residential blocks. In 2015, the St+art India Foundation, in collaboration with the Delhi government, decided this unassuming neighborhood deserved a makeover.

The result? Over 50 murals spread across residential blocks, turning a sleepy colony into one of the most Instagrammed corners of the capital, without charging a single rupee for entry.

Walls That Actually Talk to You

Massive Portraits: So detailed they look almost photographic from a distance.
Optical Illusions: Bold abstract and geometric designs that turn entire building facades inside out.
Nature-Inspired Works: Birds, trees, and wildlife bursting out of grey walls in vibrant full color.
Social Themes: Pieces exploring gender, environment, and identity that make you pause and reflect.

Pro Visit Tips for Lodhi Art District

  • Timing: Go early morning or late afternoon for the softest photography light.
  • Footwear: Wear comfortable walking shoes to cover Blocks A through WEA easily.
  • Look Everywhere: Murals wrap around multi-storey buildings — don't forget to look up!
  • Respect Locals: Keep noise down near residential quarters.

More Prominent Aesthetic Walks in Delhi

If you loved Lodhi Colony, here are three other aesthetic walks across the capital to explore next:

1. Majnu Ka Tilla (Little Tibet Walk) Narrow lanes draped with vibrant prayer flags, cozy subterranean Tibetan cafes, antique trinket shops, and the aroma of laphing and butter tea.
2. Hauz Khas Village & Lake Trail A contrast of 14th-century medieval reservoir ruins right next to trendy indie boutiques, art galleries, and rooftop cafes overlooking the lake.
3. Sundar Nursery Heritage Park Walk Delhi's premier urban park combining restored Mughal monuments, marble fountains, botanical gardens, and weekend organic artisan markets.

Why You Need to Go

In a city packed with monuments from centuries ago, Lodhi Art District is refreshingly, boldly now. It's proof that art doesn't need a frame, a ticket counter, or a velvet rope to be powerful — sometimes it just needs a blank wall and someone brave enough to paint outside the lines.
